# Benchmark 2026-10-12 — first comparison

**Data:** 40 Nutrition5k plates (overhead, depth test split, seed 5) + 2 of our posted meals.
**Accuracy:** Ollama on the Mac (M4 Pro, Metal), `vision_max_px` 896, temperature 0.1.
**Speed:** the Oracle VM (2 OCPU, CPU only), 5 Nutrition5k photos, prompt v1.

ingr. P/R: share of named ingredients that were really there / of real ingredients ≥ 5 g that
were named (both after catalog matching); mass R: recall weighted by grams. Errors are medians
of |predicted − true| / true over photos with a valid answer; kcal bias is the median signed
error (negative = underestimates). Latency includes the first photo after loading the model.

Latency and RAM in this table are the Mac's; see below for the VM.

The smaller models save only 10–20% of the time on 2 CPU cores (image encoding dominates).

## Result

- **Keep `qwen3-vl:4b-instruct` with prompt v1** as the default. Lowest kcal error (26%), best
  ingredient precision, always valid JSON. Overestimates by about a quarter (median +24%).
- `v2` (plate size + typical portions) removes that bias (−2%) but makes protein and fat worse;
  it's not adopted yet.
- `qwen3-vl:2b-instruct`: only ~20% faster on the VM and kcal off by ~80% (portions far too big).
- `qwen2.5vl:3b`: close on kcal (31%) but names fewer of the real ingredients (R 0.46), and with
  v2 43% of its answers were not valid JSON.
- Not compared: `gemma3:4b` (download failed twice on Ollama's registry), Gemma 4 (no Ollama tag
  yet), a food LoRA (none packaged for Ollama).

## Caveats

- Only 2 household meals, and one (overnight oats) was saved with just oats + milk while the photo
  shows chia, apple and honey, so the household rows say little.
- Nutrition5k is cafeteria food shot from straight above; our plates look different.
